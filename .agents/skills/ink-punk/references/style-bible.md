# Ink Punk — visual canon

## Emotional core

Playful defiance, self-possession and expressive charm define the visual world's
sensibility, not a mandatory personality or facial expression for each character.
Characters feel as if they have opinions and an existing life. The adult fashion
anchors show confident, teasing sensuality, but their half-lidded eyes, crooked
smiles, tilted heads and standing hip shifts are example performances, not
rendering invariants. Preserve requested allure through acting appropriate to
the individual: warmth, concentration, reserve, impatience or surprise can carry
it without repeating one expression.

Separate what the style keeps (ink hierarchy, black masses, cel-shadow design,
shape economy and restrained print texture) from what a new character may change
(face construction, physique, costume architecture, palette, action and camera).
Use [design range](../../../../assets/visual-project-skills/ink-punk/references/design-range.md) when creating a new identity or broadening a
series; an established identity stays stable unless the user requests a redesign.

The current human cast and fashion limits are defined in
[cast-and-fashion.md](cast-and-fashion.md). Range means variation inside that
contract: young adults 18–25, fair/pale complexion, slim/very lean physiques and
striking distinctive adult fashion. Earlier experimental older, plus-size or
strongly muscular designs are not default style anchors.

## Drawing and surface

- Confident variable-width black outlines: the silhouette and deep overlaps
  carry the strongest marks, major structural folds sit between them and fine
  face/material cues stay lighter. Do not give every strand or seam equal weight.
  Keep contours moderately weighted and tapered: the owner rejected the uniform
  heavy outlines in practice32 and42. Let connected shadow shapes carry visual
  weight instead of thickening every face, tile, window and garment boundary.
- Organize large connected solid-black or near-black shadow masses before
  microdetail, balanced by light skin, fabric and negative space. Hair reads as
  designed locks with a few decisive planes. Check the composition in grayscale
  and at thumbnail size.
- Flat local color, generally one or two crisp cel-shadow steps under coherent
  light. Angular shadows explain volume, contact and fabric tension; do not turn
  a skirt into decorative zigzags unrelated to its construction.
- Separate present materials through response rather than extra noise: matte
  rib-knit may use restrained fine lines, woven fabric broader fold planes,
  opaque hosiery smooth quiet tones, leather selected hard glints, metal tiny
  sharp accents. These are conditional examples, not a mandatory wardrobe.
- Reserve hard highlights for surfaces that warrant them. The polished boots
  in the 1.5 Lisa example are one finish, not a new universal glossy treatment.
  Skin remains softly matte; no plastic/CGI shine. Check that bright hardware
  and footwear do not displace the intended face/action focus.
- Light selective print texture may unify the finish inside the subject. Keep
  faces, hands and recognition cues crisp; avoid equal grain over all materials.
  Graphic form must remain legible without relying on surface texture.
- Polished hand-drawn illustration, with anime facial economy and exaggerated
  cartoon shape language. Avoid smooth plastic 3D shading and generic airbrush.

## Shape, silhouette and identity

Pair soft volume with sharp structure: cheeks and sleeves against lashes,
hair tips, collars and hardware. Use clear negative spaces around arms and legs.
Stylized proportions must remain consistent with the character and visibly
adult. Within the default slim/very lean build, vary height, limb proportions,
shoulder/hip relationship and subtle natural curves. Strong muscular bulk or a
plus-size build requires a specific request. No universal head-count or bust size
is prescribed. Youth does not mean childlike anatomy or an aged face on a slim body.

Hair should read as designed masses with a few decisive strands, not hundreds
of equally detailed threads. Eyes, brows and mouth carry attitude. Small fangs,
freckles, ear cuffs or unusual clips are optional character cues, not a checklist.
Choose distinctive cues that survive a small view.

## Adult fashion and allure

The [owner-supplied appeal example](../../../../assets/visual-project-skills/ink-punk/references/appeal-calibration.md) calibrates how clearly
sexual the adult female fashion branch should read, including covered outfits.
Keep its reference role separate from our rendering anchors. Pleasantness,
polish and design variety alone do not meet this emotional target.

The approved direction uses striking, varied and sensuous young-adult fashion as
an active part of the character. The outfit should have an intentional design
idea, rather than functioning only as a neutral occupation label. Correct ink alone
can still leave a dull or unalluring character. Check the requested adult appeal
through the complete look; closed fashion can satisfy it, as practice07 shows:


- A fitted region may contrast a broad jacket, loose sleeve, voluminous trouser
  leg, long skirt or heavy footwear.
- Tailoring and drape reveal the waist/hip relationship without relying on nudity.
- Asymmetric necklines, a bare shoulder, a deliberate slit, opaque stockings,
  a small strip of midriff or a choker can support the brief. None is mandatory.
- Poses communicate agency through the actual action and attitude. A hip shift,
  head tilt and eye contact are available examples, not the default pose recipe.
  Stillness, concentration, work, movement and reaction can express the same
  character's appeal. A broad smile can also be sensual; do not ban it because
  one friendly standing sprite failed. Choose support, action and gaze before
  decorative gestures, then check their actual combined effect.
- Match clothing coverage to the brief and setting; keep gravity, seams and
  anatomy plausible. Designed lingerie looks, a visible bralette edge, hosiery,
  cutouts or sheer outer layers can belong to the requested adult fashion/homewear
  direction. Choose them intentionally where they support the look. Preserve
  an established outfit when it is locked by the brief; adding sexual acts or
  accidentally undressing the character does not solve its design.

Do not recycle the same asymmetrical top and dropped jacket for every new design.
Explore fitted tailoring, flowing fabric, broad silhouettes, long coats or compact
layering while maintaining the same visual logic and requested sensuality.
Use the garment-family examples in [cast and fashion](cast-and-fashion.md) when
the brief is open; do not repeat one cropped top/jacket/skirt recipe for everyone.

## Color roles

A striking look need not use bright or saturated colors. Black, white, monochrome
and muted palettes can be equally memorable through silhouette, cut, material
contrast, detail placement and acting. Vivid color remains one available choice.

Use relationships, not a universal hue prescription:

| Role | Canonical examples |
| --- | --- |
| Structural dark | Ink black, charcoal, deep muted teal |
| Light/rest | Warm ivory, pale skin, silver hair, cream fabric |
| Dominant character accent | Coral bag, acid-lime jacket, violet detail |
| Secondary accent | Pale mint, warm amber, restrained plum |
| Background atmosphere | Muted lavender, desaturated teal, warm paper |

Pick a clear main accent and, when useful, a subordinate accent. Let a different
character own a different palette. Do not make every surface equally saturated.
Preserve local color under scene lighting instead of washing the whole image
into an undifferentiated purple or orange filter.

## Composition and environments

Standalone characters, dialogue assets and expression variants use a transparent
background by default. The warm paper and oval shapes in older anchors are
presentation context, not mandatory style elements. Never transfer them to a
character asset. Apply texture only inside the illustrated subject.

A character image with a background is a composed visual-novel CG/art branch.
There the requested environment or deliberate art backdrop can be present.
Do not add a backdrop merely because a style reference has one.

For a game location, extend the same shape economy to architecture: chunky pipes,
clear frames, angular awnings, railings, utility boxes and selective large motifs.
These are examples from the street anchor, not requirements for a forest or room.

The night street anchor pairs amber windows, violet arcade light, muted teal
distance and wet pavement. Preserve its **organization of light and depth** when
changing location, rather than adding neon to every scene. Daylight is allowed;
keep the same graphic treatment and focal hierarchy.

Figures occupy a coherent ground plane with contact/cast shadows. Ambient light,
rim light, reflected color and perspective agree across people and objects.
Use lower detail/contrast in distance and preserve playable-space readability.
When the character/gesture is the focus, secondary architecture must not take
over through equally crisp detail and contrast (practice37/38). A setting or
story clue can itself be the focus; keep it clear when that is the job. This is
not a universal blur requirement.
Character scale follows the requested camera; an establishing view needs more
environment than a fashion portrait. Do not claim gameplay readability from a
large poster alone.

## Drift to correct

- A generic anime face with no particular attitude.
- A realistic background behind flat pasted-on character cutouts.
- Uniformly thin contours or texture replacing purposeful black masses.
- Glossy airbrushed skin, soft gradients everywhere, CGI material treatment.
- Fold shadows becoming ornamental zigzags, uniform grain hiding material
  differences, or hard highlights spreading to every surface.
- Too many equally loud accents, glowing outlines or tiny accessories.
- Identical costumes, poses and body types on supposedly different characters.
- Every character becoming chibi or childlike in an adult fashion brief.
- Adult fashion losing all flirtation despite the requested mood, or adding
  explicit exposure in place of designing clothes and body language.
- Faces hidden by decorative lighting; shoes floating or shadows disagreeing.
- New typography, logos, watermarks or UI elements that were not requested.
- A white/colored field, oval, scenery or baked exterior shadow behind a requested
  standalone game character; a checkerboard painted into the image instead of alpha.
